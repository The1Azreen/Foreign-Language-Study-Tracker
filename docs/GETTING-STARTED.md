# Getting started: your first month (December)

This is a step-by-step guide for an absolute beginner, starting **Monday 1 December 2026**, using Anki and this tracker.

- **Part 1** gets everything ready before December (about 1 hour in total).
- **Part 2** is the daily routine.
- **Part 3** is a week-by-week plan for December.
- **Part 4** covers what to do when things go wrong.

---

## Part 1 — Before 1 December (do this in November)

### 1. Tracker settings (already done)
These are already set in **Settings**:
- Start date: `2026-12-01`. This makes December *Month 1* in the **Year 1** tab.
- Daily immersion goal: **60 min**
- Daily new cards goal: **20**. This is for kana in weeks 1–2; change it to **10** on 15 December.

To open the tracker, double-click `start-tracker.bat`. Open Anki first so the Anki stats load.

### 2. Anki deck options (10 min)
In Anki, click the ⚙ next to a deck → **Options**. Decks can share an options *preset*, so give Kaishi its own: use the preset dropdown at the top → **Add preset** → name it `Kaishi`.

| Setting | Kana deck | Kaishi 1.5k deck |
|---|---|---|
| New cards/day | **20** | **0** until 15 Dec, then **10** |
| Maximum reviews/day | 200 | 200 |
| Learning steps | `1m 10m` | `1m 10m` |
| FSRS (Advanced section) | **On** | **On** |
| Desired retention | 0.90 | 0.90 |

With 20 new cards a day, the Kana deck (222 cards) takes about **11 days**. The hiragana cards come first, then katakana.

### 3. Anki on your phone (15 min)
Install **AnkiDroid** (Android, free) or **AnkiMobile** (iPhone, paid). Create an **AnkiWeb** account and click **Sync** on desktop, then log in and sync on the phone.

Doing reviews on your phone in spare moments (on the bus, in queues) is the easiest way to never miss a day. The first sync can take a while because of Kaishi's audio files.

### 4. Yomitan (10 min)
You won't need it until month 3–4, but set it up now while it's fresh:
1. Add Yomitan to Chrome.
2. Import the 4 dictionaries from `data/yomitan-dictionaries` (Settings → Dictionaries → Import).
3. Test it: on any Japanese site, hold **Shift** and hover a word.

### 5. Pick your listening (10 min)
Bookmark 2–3 things now so you don't have to decide every day:
- **Comprehensible Japanese** (YouTube): made for complete beginners, with drawings and very simple Japanese. This is the best fit for December.
- **Nihongo con Teppei for beginners** (podcast): short episodes.
- **An anime you already like**: put it on with Japanese audio. Understanding nothing is fine; you're getting used to the sounds.

Add them to the tracker's **Library** tab. Then the ▶ button starts a timer for that item.

### 6. Watch Stephen Krashen (late November, 1 hour)
Search "Stephen Krashen Language Acquisition" on YouTube. It explains *why* this plan is mostly listening and reading, and that makes it easier to trust the process when it feels slow.

## Part 2 — Your daily routine (about 60 min)

| When | What | Time | How to track it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morning / commute | **Anki reviews first, then new cards.** Always finish reviews; new cards are optional on a bad day. | 15–25 min | Log as *Anki / SRS*. Anki shows "studied X cards in Y min" on its main screen. |
| Any time | **Kana quiz** in the tracker's Learn tab, only in weeks 1–2 | 5–10 min | The **Log practice time** button under the quiz |
| Evening | **Listening/watching**: something from your Library | 30+ min | Start the **Quick timer** on the Dashboard, then **Stop & log** |
| From week 3 | **Grammar**: one Cure Dolly video | 10–15 min | Log as *Grammar study* |

**Before bed (2 min):** open the tracker →
1. Anki tab → **Refresh stats** (this also saves today's snapshot for your charts).
2. Dashboard → check that today's bar is full and your streak is safe.
3. Tick any Year 1 tasks you finished.

> **Rule of thumb:** if you only have 10 minutes, do your Anki reviews. If you have zero minutes, listen to Japanese while doing something else and log it. Keep the streak alive.

## Part 3 — December, week by week

### Week 1 · 1–7 Dec · Hiragana
- **Goal:** read all 46 basic hiragana. By the end of the week you've also met the ゛゜ forms and combinations like きゃ.
- **Anki:** Kana deck, 20 new a day. Press **Again** honestly when you don't know a card.
- **Learn tab:** read the hiragana chart once a day. Quiz on *Hiragana → Basic*, then add *Dakuten* around day 4.
- **Tip:** for each kana, look up the picture mnemonic in Tofugu's hiragana guide (tofugu.com/japanese/learn-hiragana). It makes the cards much easier.
- **Listening:** 30 min a day of Comprehensible Japanese.
- **Year 1 tasks to tick:** *Watch Krashen*, *Learn all hiragana*.

### Week 2 · 8–14 Dec · Katakana + sounds
- **Goal:** read all katakana, and understand how Japanese sounds work.
- **Anki:** the Kana deck switches to katakana on its own (cards are in that order). Keep 20 new a day; it should finish around 11–12 Dec.
- **Learn tab:** quiz on *Katakana*, then *Both*. Read the **Sound rules** section at the bottom of the tab and say the examples out loud.
- **Pitch accent:** watch Kaname Naito's "Basics of Japanese Pitch Accent" once, then take the kotu.io test (link in Resources). Write your score in the notes of a log entry; you'll compare it in month 9.
- **Year 1 tasks:** *Learn all katakana*, *Sounds*, *Naito video*, *kotu.io baseline*.
- **Check:** can you read こんにちは, ありがとう, コーヒー and ゲーム without the chart? If yes, you're ready for week 3.

### Week 3 · 15–21 Dec · First words
- **Anki:** set Kaishi's **New cards/day to 10**. In the tracker's Settings, change *Daily new cards goal* to **10**.
- On each Kaishi card, play the audio and read the example sentence. Don't worry about grammar yet.
- **Grammar:** Cure Dolly's "Japanese from Scratch" videos 1–3. Watch to understand; there's nothing to memorize.
- Keep listening 30+ min a day. You'll start hearing words from your deck in the wild, and that's the moment it starts to click.
- **Year 1 tasks:** *Start Kaishi*, *Cure Dolly 1–5* (in progress).

### Week 4 · 22–31 Dec · Build the habit
- Keep the same routine: Kaishi 10 a day, all reviews, Cure Dolly videos 4–5, listening.
- **Holiday tip:** if travel or family time eats your schedule, do *only* Anki reviews on your phone (15 min). It's fine to skip new cards for a few days.
- **New Year's Eve:** Japan's 紅白歌合戦 (Kōhaku, an end-of-year music show) is on 31 Dec. It's fun, seasonal listening practice.
- **31 Dec, monthly review (15 min):** open the tracker →
  - **Year 1 → Month 1:** how many tasks are ticked? Are you at the target of **25 hours**?
  - **Dashboard:** look at the heatmap. Which days were empty, and why?
  - **Anki tab:** what's your 30-day retention? Below 80% means too many new cards, so lower them. Above 90% with time to spare means you can go up to 15.
  - Write 2–3 sentences in a log note about what worked, then roll into **Month 2** (January).

## Part 4 — When things go wrong

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Reviews pile up (100+ due)** | Set new cards to 0 for a few days and only clear reviews. Never "reset" the deck. |
| **Can't remember a kana after many tries** | Look up its mnemonic again, and use the Learn tab quiz. It shows the ones you miss more often. |
| **Missed a day** | Do the reviews the next day, even if there are a lot. The streak restarts, but the progress doesn't. |
| **Listening feels pointless** | Normal for the first months. Switch to the easiest content (Comprehensible Japanese), and treat it as getting used to the sounds rather than understanding. |
| **Want to see today's cards again** | Tools → Create Filtered Deck → search `deck:Kana rated:1` → untick "Reschedule cards". |
| **Tracker shows no Anki stats** | Anki must be open. Then Anki tab → Refresh stats. |
